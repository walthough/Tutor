# Basis for language teaching choices

Use these sources for rationale, not as a script to recite during lessons.

- [Nation (2007), The Four Strands](https://www.victoria.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0019/1626121/2007-Four-strands.pdf): a course-design framework balancing meaning-focused input, output, deliberate study and fluency. Supports checking the balance across sessions; it does not establish a uniquely optimal timetable for this learner.
- [Karpicke & Roediger (2008), The Critical Importance of Retrieval for Learning](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1152408): foreign-language vocabulary experiments found benefits of continued retrieval for delayed recall. Supports retrieving familiar words again rather than retiring them after one success; vocabulary-pair recall alone does not establish communicative ability.
- [Cepeda et al. (2006), Distributed Practice in Verbal Recall Tasks](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/): synthesis supports distributed over massed practice, with timing dependent on the retention goal. Use existing queues and adapt reviews; a fixed app interval is not validated by this finding alone.
- [Li (2010), The Effectiveness of Corrective Feedback in SLA](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x): meta-analysis supports corrective feedback, with variation across conditions. Choose feedback to suit communication and focused practice; no universal correction style is implied.
- [Council of Europe (2020), CEFR Companion Volume](https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4): descriptors cover reception, production, interaction and mediation. Use them to sample capabilities; the framework is not evidence that quiz accuracy or a practice meter yields CEFR placement.

The particular task loop, correction priorities and adaptation rules are practical design choices informed by these sources. Their effectiveness in this tutor has not been measured.
